### B2. Direct-origin resolution can expand paths before the SCC budget

In the [fencing scanner](../../tests/unit/architecture/test_web_landscape_mutation_fencing.py#L10281),

root budget at 12595. Graph transfer also calls it at 12475–12476 and 12515.

Discriminating source shape, inside one function:

### B3. Discovery, overlapping capture subtrees and origin width are outside counted work

**Bounded residual limitation unless measurement makes it a Task 0 blocker.**

[RetentionGraph.evaluate](../../tests/helpers/web_reserve_retention.py#L38)
materializes dependency tuples and traverses graph edges at 49–71; its first
charge occurs only at transfer (88 or 109). Consequently a low transfer cap
does not cap discovered vertices, edges or allocations. In the fencing
scanner, a read of a multiply assigned name materializes every producer's
specification (12280–12362). Separate read AST identities repeat that
producer fanout. Capture discovery walks the callable subtree
(12266–12278), but **`retained_capture_inputs` is cached at 12265**.
Capture transfer at 12410–12411 reuses that owner's cached input tuple;
re-evaluating the same owner during an SCC does not repeat its AST scan.
The earlier report's claim of an uncached same-owner rescan was incorrect
and is withdrawn. The decorator is also present at 12261 of predecessor
`a6c0aba2cf680d3db6371f9bae37cd9e532da652`.

The corpus decorator preserves this cache: it first applies `original_cache`
and returns that wrapper unchanged for non-root helpers
([corpus:106–111](../../scripts/fencing_retention_corpus.py#L106)). The
test factory does likewise at fixpoint-test 33–44. The remaining potential
cost is distinct nested callable owners walking overlapping subtrees on
their respective first discoveries, plus transfer iteration over an already
cached capture-input tuple. Neither cost has been measured here.

The graph's edge cache prevents repeating completed discovery for the same
key, and final states suppress completed dependency work. Those are real
controls. Failed SCCs publish no final values, while edges remain cached;
later consumers may traverse/re-evaluate the unfinished closure. This is
bounded per counted consumer, not a unit-wide deadline.

Origin work has the same distinction. A transfer can union or map a broad
origin set for one charge (12419–12448, 12454–12471). Replay
[lines 163–165](../../tests/helpers/web_reserve_retention.py#L160) sorts all
choices and allocates one copied dictionary per alternative before the next
charge. Thus the aggregate cap bounds attempted combinations and transfers,
not bytes, edge visits or origin-element operations.

**Smallest probes:** a low-cap long chain before its first transfer; distinct
nested callable owners with overlapping subtrees; many assignments/read
sites of one name; a one-factor wide origin set with a cap of one. Count
capture-cache misses and first-discovery AST visits separately from cache
hits, alongside dependency visits and pending alternatives. All are unrun.
If required by measurements, charge incremental discovery/origin work before
large materialization. Preserve the existing per-unit/pass capture cache;
adding that cache is not an outstanding fix.
Do not raise the existing cap or publish a failed graph as successful to
hide expensive work.

**Resolved counterexample:** candidate replay caches each projected factor
at helper 145/153–154, and graph charges and replay attempts share the
consumer count at fencing 12521–12529 and 13198–13218. This addresses the
prior repeated-abandoned-root multiplication within one consumer. It is
not a new finding here.

## Work and retention relationships

There is no evidence requiring a sociological archetype. The relevant
mechanisms are concrete:

- More unresolved roots cause more traversal of unfinished closures; without
  successful finalization, that work does not reduce the unresolved stock.
  Final caching breaks this repetition for completed components; per-consumer
  fallback caching now breaks it within replay. Discovery and width remain
  outside that balancing control.
- More path-distinct resolver cache entries retain more `seen` sets and
  results without useful reuse across distinct contexts. This is a
  per-pass accumulation mechanism, not evidence of a process-lifetime leak.
- Hard worker exit removes a stuck worker but skips cleanup that ordinarily
  drains its child-process stock. External ownership/termination is the
  missing companion control; raising timeout values does not supply it.
- Parameterized reserve tests reconstruct a proof for each safe/mutated
  corpus, for example contracts 5709–5720. `_reserve_run` creates a new
  proof (fencing 20263–20264), whose constructor calls this scanner again
  (18033–18038). Faster roots do not by themselves prove acceptable file or
  suite duration. Do not add cross-pass caches keyed only by path to optimize
  this: same-AST mutation tests at contracts 6517–6542 require fresh semantics.
