## Three biggest risks

1. **The zero-failure bar is unreachable in the window without a ruling on the nine spec-ahead
   ids.** Two thirds of Phase 4's hours sit in three programs the platform branch's own tests
   declared RED by design (Task 5, Task 6, database-clock authority). Every other id is ~115 h.
   Deciding late strands nothing but the schedule; deciding "execute" without saying so silently
   triples Phase 4.
2. **One production seam, four writers, one signing window.** Blob-context threading (C1/C4)
   touches `web/execution/service.py`, `guided.py`, `source_inspection.py`; D3 and 6b-2 add
   authorities; identity-sprint adds 5 writers and 11 tables; `sessions/service.py` carries 14
   signed keys. Semantic merges here are exactly the class git auto-merges silently — pre-commit
   mypy is the detector, and the writer-manifest (#10) corpus moves under D1/D6 while it is open.
   Phase 5 staged before this settles is a wasted operator round.
3. **Fail-open test doubles are the class that just bit staging twice.** Lane A creates a lease
   helper over a recording authority; lane B widens ten monkeypatched settlement doubles; the two
   live-trial defects were invisible precisely because doubles carried defaults. Each helper ships
   with its propagation test, and the `7e34eef6c` tree-wide sweep for callers omitting a
   default-less fence kwarg is rerun at the end of every wave — otherwise the third such defect is
   found on Azure, on a ticket that forbids waiving evidence.
