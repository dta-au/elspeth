## Decision and boundary

The immediate decision is how to repair ordinary continuation cuts that stop
working when a segment ends between completed actions, without broadening
provider replay or building another state-authority framework. The boundary
is the persisted continuation from a real producer through action selection,
drain, suspension, claim/start, cold restoration and publication. The horizon
is the current 0.8.10 repair and its next maintainable implementation, not a
future architecture rewrite.

**The useful leverage is consistency between the existing source owners and
their consumers.** The drain classifier is only one consumer. A whitelist
patch can admit an unproved source or move a legitimate request to the next
reader that rejects it. A universal recovery preprocessor would create the
opposite error: imposing semantic-source prerequisites on Initialize, which
has no semantic event to normalize. Both failure directions are present.

The threat model here includes accidental developer/operator mistakes and
untrusted external inputs. It does not assume malicious insiders. Existing
receipt, source, ownership and accounting checks matter because they keep
paid work and publication correct; no new state permits, signatures,
organizational gates or historical-format adapters are recommended.

## Prioritized findings

### P1: ordinary PRIMARY graph-recovery Stop lacks recovery normalization

This is an additional source mismatch beyond the supplied F33–F35 findings.
In [semantic advisor SQL](../../src/elspeth/web/sessions/continuation_semantic_advisor.py),
`_consume_semantic_recovery_stop_from_admission` at lines 2858–2923 consumes a
real paid Stop over an ordinary PRIMARY recovery intent, writes the candidate
with the paid semantic source and emits `semantic_ordinary_recovery_stop_ready`
at END `publication_ready`. `is_consumed_semantic_stop_cut` recognizes that
candidate, and the drain classifier explicitly admits consumed whole Stops.

After a real suspension, `_semantic_ordinary_recovery_publication_data`
at lines 2945–3020 uses `selected.cursor - 1` as the installed Stop snapshot.
It does not first resolve the recovered suffix. With one recovery, that
predecessor is the consumed END result, not the preceding installed Advisor
decision. `_reprove_installed_snapshot` cannot prove it as an installed
Advisor. Consecutive recoveries preserve the same mismatch. The reader also
expects the Stop event at the selected cursor, which is now `recovered`.

The public wrapper retains correct current-fence/CAS checks at lines
2926–2942; the defect is inside its immutable history interpretation.
Production invokes it during cold result preparation, final SQL and graph
recovery selection, as linked above. Therefore merely getting the driver
past initial restoration would not close this path.

Counterevidence helps isolate the repair: clean and owned iteration
publication readers at lines 2298–2305 and 2508–2513 already normalize with
`semantic_recovery_source`. Reviewed-END publication does so in
[semantic actions](../../src/elspeth/web/sessions/continuation_semantic_actions.py),
lines 1352–1354. This is uneven adoption of an existing contract, not missing
generic recovery machinery.

Small repair: after establishing this reader's applicable END/recovery
domain, normalize its selected historical source with the existing strict
helper, then preserve its exact original candidate, paid Stop, event and
result checks. Current admission and all writes remain bound to the real
current head. No paid call should repeat.

The inspected [ordinary recovery test](../../tests/unit/web/sessions/test_continuation_semantic_owned_publication.py),
lines 224–329, runs real `handoff_to_user`/park plus paid Stop, completes it,
then cold-reads the historical Stop checkpoint. It does not suspend between
Stop consumption and publication. The [handover tests](../../tests/unit/web/sessions/test_continuation_semantic_handover.py)
at lines 322 and 401 cover applied-iteration and reviewed-END publication,
respectively. Their bodies establish useful controls but do not establish
this distinct ordinary PRIMARY route. This is a statement about inspected
tests, not a whole-repository coverage count.

Falsifiable control: reach that genuine ordinary recovery Stop, perform
actual suspend/claim/start, then finish original graph recovery and final
publication. Repeat with consecutive recoveries. Assert original PRIMARY
and paid Stop identities and result, unchanged request/slice counters at the
recovery edges, no repeated provider call, and current-owner finalization.
Change the original Stop/candidate or a real recovery event/attempt pair and
require refusal without partial graph-recovery or publication writes. Native
failure and passing repair remain Root-owned and unmeasured by this review.

### P1: F33–F35 form one producer/consumer consistency problem

The reported mismatches remain visible in the inspected source:

* F33: the real completed Review phases above are accepted by the semantic
  owner but excluded by `live_later_cut` in drain lines 79–90. Structural
  admission's quiescent tail at next-action lines 517–521 does not invoke the
  exact completed Review source reader. Extending the pure filter alone is
  incomplete; source admission must accompany it.
* F34: [Read execution](../../src/elspeth/web/composer/continuation_semantic_advisor_phase.py),
  lines 239–277, intentionally returns its committed result before a later
  SQL consume action. [Read SQL](../../src/elspeth/web/sessions/continuation_semantic_actions.py),
  lines 737–785, treats the current snapshot as the original commit. The
  advisor SQL consume at lines 3072–3103 does the same, while its cold bridge
  reader at lines 3156–3175 requires `consume_cursor == commit_cursor + 1`.
  Real recovery legitimately inserts another cursor. The original commit
  and current consume predecessor must remain distinct identities, joined
  by the exact proved recovery path, not an arbitrary gap.
* F35: advisor SQL lines 3361–3366 asks for a semantic `kind` before accepting
  the normalized original returned-iteration event, whose current format is
  stage-owned instead. Its descendant recursion at lines 3401–3461 also
  requires semantic event interpretation at each predecessor, including
  recovered predecessors. Normalizing only the first entry is insufficient.
  The Read descendant's `commit_cursor + 1` assumption at line 3455 is a
  downstream instance of F34.

The smallest coherent functional repair is the existing G01 plan plus the
ordinary-recovery publication case above. Keep original committed DATA
unchanged, consume from the actual current head, and reprove every accepted
recovery edge. Do not rewrite stored history, widen to `consume > commit`,
or extend same-sequence logic to `yielded` without the existing separate
slice/source contract.

### P1: a recovery helper can also be applied too early

The database reviewer reported an actual Initialize failure in the R16012
controls; this review independently inspected the implicated source, not
the native receipt. [END retry phase](../../src/elspeth/web/sessions/continuation_checkpoint_end_retry.py),
lines 891–934, calls `semantic_recovery_source` at line 898 before deciding
whether the frame/event belongs to END retry. Its source reader requires an
event at the selected cursor and raises if none exists. The generic
next-action startup path calls this optional retry probe through lines
1333–1335 of continuation SQL; the database review's observed failure enters
it earlier, during suspension through `_retained_successor_action`. The
initializer at continuation SQL lines 271–283 writes a genuine cursor-zero
checkpoint without a journal event. That initial checkpoint has
no semantic event, so semantic recovery's own strict preconditions are
inappropriate for that optional probe.

This changes the systemic recommendation: **check source-domain
applicability before applying its strict reader; within that domain,
normalize recovery before interpreting the original producer event.** A
nonapplicable optional probe returns `None`; a malformed applicable source
still raises. Do not fix this by making the strict semantic normalizer accept
arbitrary missing events or by treating every integrity error as absence.
Do not reject the test merely because it starts at Initialize.

Controls must pair actual initial reservation and its optional non-END
probe with actual recovered END retry. Corrupt an applicable retry marker
and retain refusal. This distinguishes domain selection from weakening the
owned reader. Prefer the narrow applicable-END check in
`read_retry_ready_action_source_on_connection`, whose contract is END-only;
the underlying phase reader also serves iteration-END and must retain that
domain. Keep genuine Initialize handover tests. Tests that specifically
claim to exercise semantic recovery attempt-pair rejection must instead
reach an actual semantic/END source so another rejection cannot mask the
intended condition. Root and the database reviewer own the native evidence.

### P1: startup reconciliation requires running authority before admitting no start

The same ordering problem appears across a different boundary. The database
reviewer identified it independently; this review inspected the implicated
producer, reconciler, validator and worker bodies. In
[repository startup](../../src/elspeth/web/coordination/repository.py), lines
5803–5807, the real observation captures the provisional context before
`start_segment_on_connection` finishes. If that transaction rolls back, the
observation can retain its context while persisted start history is absent.

`reconcile_composer_segment_start` at lines 5829–5837 first demands current
running authority for that context and catches only `ComposerOperationFenceLost`.
[Current validation](../../src/elspeth/web/sessions/continuation.py), lines
2841–2850, raises `AuditIntegrityError` when continuation mutation authority
does not exist. Consequently the existing `history is None` plus exact
unstarted-segment branch at repository lines 5838–5858 is not reached in
this rollback scenario. The [worker](../../src/elspeth/web/sessions/composer_async_worker.py),
lines 403–417 and 453–459, expressly needs a reconciled `None` to mark this
claim known-unstarted and release it. A provisional in-memory context is
being treated as a premise that SQL start must already exist.

Small repair: establish the actual persisted start/no-start case under the
existing session transaction before choosing the corresponding validator.
Preserve exact claim, token, generation and observed-context consistency;
contradictory persisted history or a changed claim must still refuse. Do not
catch all `AuditIntegrityError` and convert them to no start. Retain genuine
rollback-at-start and committed-start readback controls, including competing
ownership and contradictory-candidate opponents. This is a reconciliation
contract repair, not a reason to weaken current running authority.

### P2: duplicate projections invite drift; consolidate at existing boundaries

`handoff_cut_allowance` in next-action lines 601–650 says its END phases
map to Advisor, PRIMARY and SQL. The main next-action function instead
handles END first and correctly chooses Advisor until consumed Stop.
Its early END return makes those helper branches irrelevant to normal END
allowance execution; the drain caller uses their non-`None` result as a
capability signal. The automatic successor caller has already restricted
itself to Compose. This is contradictory maintenance guidance and mixed
responsibility, not evidence that the current driver runs PRIMARY directly
after a flagged Review.

A bounded cleanup would keep one existing next-action owner for actual
allowance and make drain consume the relevant source-proved classification
without a second speculative END phase-to-action mapping. No new generic
registry is required. Preserve explicit unsupported entered/held effects;
their refusal is not a missing whitelist entry.

The publication call chain similarly tries several source readers in order.
The iteration readers each normalize the same history before determining
their own event kind. This establishes repeated work in source, but not its
runtime cost. After correctness, a same-transaction lexical observation can
select the applicable publication source once and feed its existing reader.
Do not cache successful current admission across commits, cursor changes or
ownership changes. Measure query work and wall time on genuine equivalent
flows before claiming a speed improvement; this review made neither
measurement. A large cache would conceal inconsistent contracts and add
invalidation work without resolving them.

## Minimal causal model and intervention comparison

Observed structure: business phases and journal events carry different
information. Recovery preserves business state but adds a journal event and
changes ownership. Consumers independently project that combined state into
allowance, drain disposition, original source and next action. Some consumers
interpret a semantic phase as enough; others assume physical cursor adjacency;
another probes a semantic event before establishing its domain.

The explanatory hypothesis is that separately maintained projections and
tests at different boundary depths allow those local assumptions to drift.
The direct local bugs are established by the cited code; the development
feedback explanation is a hypothesis. An alternative is an intentional
unsupported recovery policy, but the writers, generic consumed-Stop drain
acceptance and existing recovered publication readers contradict that
explanation for the cases identified here. Intentional unsupported in-flight
effects remain outside the claim.

| Intervention | Expected effect | Cost or unwanted consequence | Discriminating observation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No change | Same outputs remain incompatible at the inspected recovery boundaries. | Failures remain timing/drain dependent. | Genuine reached-cut recovery reproduces or falsifies each predicted rejection. |
| Add drain phase names only | Completed Review passes the first filter. | Does not establish actual Review source or repair cold readers. | Alter the actual Review source while retaining its phase; classification must reject. |
| Increase quantum or broaden automatic rollover | May avoid a particular handover in a particular run. | Does not fix drain/cold interpretation; rollover also changes slice semantics. | Force real process handover at the retained cut; the mismatch remains. |
| Normalize every snapshot before every probe | Helps applicable semantic history. | Rejects legitimate initial/nonsemantic sources; can conflate `yielded` with same-sequence recovery. | Actual Initialize and recovered END retry must both behave correctly. |
| Repair the existing source owners and remove redundant projections | Preserves exact lineage while making real writer outputs consumable across handover. | Requires touching the complete downstream chain and testing family-specific controls. | Same genuine source completes with zero, one and consecutive recoveries, while changed source/event/owner is rejected. |
| Add broad state framework or authority cache | No demonstrated need here. | More dispatch/invalidation rules and likely more competing interpretations. | No measured benefit supplied; do not choose this before the bounded repair. |

The most useful test pattern is a small family-specific continuation law:
produce the real retained result; run its ordinary next action; separately
produce it and traverse real suspend/claim/start before that action; require
the same business result and original paid/read identities. Then exercise a
later real consumer, not just the first reader. Cursor/generation differences
are expected; arbitrary source differences are not. Current owner, Stop,
rollback and competing-claim controls remain necessary. File-backed SQLite
does not prove PostgreSQL contention behavior.

This uses existing fixture chains and production writers. It does not require
an exhaustive new matrix, state permit layer, synthetic attempt generator or
tests that merely mirror classifier branches. One real elapsed completion
control complements the targeted handovers: it checks the timing trigger and
ordinary completion together, while targeted controls locate late boundaries
that a single elapsed run may never reach.
